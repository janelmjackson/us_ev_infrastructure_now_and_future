# United State's EV Infrastructure: Now and Future
With the increase of demand for EV’s, there will be a commensurate increase in demand for supporting infrastructure. This project evaluates available data to determine who is being served by the current infrastructure investment and who faces the largest barriers to entry in the current marketplace. Regional differences, such as grid capacity,
energy sources, and population density involving the existing infrastructure,should be investigated to provide data driven insights on important decisions about the future transportation infrastructure.

The original analyses and project report were completed on February 17th, 2025, by Jesse Garcia, Janel Jackson, and Jason Rushton for SIADS 593 – Capstone I in the University of Michigan Master of Applied Data Science program. Unless otherwise specified in a commit, all repository updates after February 17th, 2025, were made by Janel Jackson.

## Questions evaluated in Study
1. Are there currently enough charging stations within the U.S. to charge all electric vehicles?
2. If there is a deficit, how many additional EV chargers per start are needed?
3. How much money is needed to increase the infrastructure to meet the EV demand?
4. Where should infrastructure improvements prioritize not only increasing EV charging availability but also modernizing the energy grid to maximize the benefits of EV adoption?
5. Do some states see more benefits from EV adoption than others due to non-renewable resources?
